# Research Editorial Standard

Applies to daily market notes, bot explanations, dashboards, agent handoffs and research
readouts. Facts, models, allocation rules and execution gates are unchanged by this standard.

## The Reader's Job

A reader should leave with a view of the market, the evidence that matters, the change from
the prior observation, the model's actual response, and the conditions that would change
the assessment. A list of indicators is source material, not a finished research note.

The public 42 Macro sample separates current conditions, time horizons and recurring themes.
AQR's public research starts with a concrete investment question and examines implementation
and contrary evidence. Those are useful editorial practices. We do not reproduce their
language, adopt their forecasts, or imply access to their private models.

## Writing Rules

Lead with the assessment. Name the asset, comparison period and evidence. "QQQ fell while
equal-weight stocks gained" says more than "markets present a nuanced picture."

Put numbers in the argument. Distinguish a return from a relative return, a drawdown from
a forecast, and an observation date from a release date. Model-generated prose uses bound
fact tokens so the renderer inserts verified values. Unsupported numbers remain prohibited.

Prioritize changes. A persistent view can remain unchanged. Do not manufacture a fresh
narrative each day. Explain an actual change in exposure using the native rule and schedule;
an active monitor alone is not evidence that the strategy traded.

Use descriptive headings where the evidence warrants them. No required three-part essay,
numbered insight list, artificial bull/bear symmetry, or concluding restatement. A short note
is appropriate when little has changed. A complicated disagreement deserves more space.

Keep the counterargument specific. Sector participation can challenge a broad equity-stress
view. An observed credit spread can confirm or contradict a fund-price proxy. "There are
risks on both sides" is not an adequate counterargument.

State the revision conditions. Give observable thresholds or named data that would change
the assessment. These are research conditions, not newly invented native trading triggers.

Consolidate recurring methodology and risk disclosures. Keep material data failures visible.
Do not append a disclaimer to every sentence or replace analysis with warnings.

Avoid generic introductions, hype, vague intensifiers, metaphors, canned optimism, and
repeated "not X but Y" rhetoric. Use ordinary financial language. A phrase blacklist catches
some regressions; it cannot certify reasoning, factual support or editorial quality.

## Review Before Publication

- Does the lead state a view, rather than announce that a summary follows?
- Are the important figures and horizons included in the explanation?
- Can every material assertion be traced to a source fact or recorded model rule?
- Are the strongest contrary observation and revision conditions clear?
- Is model behavior distinguished from the writer's interpretation and from actual holdings?
- Could this paragraph appear unchanged on almost any market day? If so, replace it or omit it.
- Do charts answer a stated question, with definitions available outside the main argument?

The deterministic note, written readout and dashboard share one copy generator. No external
model connector is enabled. An optional agent's prose still requires bound evidence,
identified review, numerical validation and editorial checks; no claim of automated
independent analyst review is made.
