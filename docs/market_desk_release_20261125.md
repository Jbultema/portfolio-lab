## Interpretation

Technology damage and defensive-sector relative leadership are visible. Current revised
growth data are not uniformly recessionary: industrial production is up about 1.66% year on
year and payrolls rose 411,000 over three months. Meanwhile, high-yield OAS widened 0.54
percentage points over twenty observations and Fed assets declined about 0.18% over four
weekly observations. These observations have different timestamps and do not establish a
causal market explanation or a calibrated crash forecast.

At the November 25 close, SPY's twenty-one-session return is -6.88%, QQQ is 16.55%
below its sixty-three-session high, and six of nine stable sector funds remain above their
two-hundred-session averages. Staples, healthcare and utilities lead relative to SPY.
This is meaningful counterevidence to an indiscriminate "everything is breaking" narrative,
not a guarantee that rotation baskets will protect capital.

The proper reading is not "all bots agree to exit everything." The technology control has
reduced exposure; the slower ETF/sector models retain their native sector baskets; the stock
shadow is data-blocked. Research targets, saved baseline behavior and actual personal holdings
must remain distinct.
